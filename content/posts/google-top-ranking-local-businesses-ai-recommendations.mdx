Ranking near the top of Google has traditionally been one of the biggest goals of local SEO. But customers are no longer discovering businesses through Google’s traditional search results alone.

They can now ask AI-powered search tools questions such as:

> “What are the best digital marketing agencies in Dubai?”

That raised an interesting question for us at LeadsPro:

If a business performs well in traditional Google Search, does that automatically mean it will also be surfaced when AI systems recommend local businesses?

We ran an exploratory test using digital marketing agencies in Dubai to better understand the relationship between traditional search visibility and AI-driven discovery.

Here’s what we learned.

## How We Conducted the Experiment

We tested variations of commercially relevant searches around digital marketing services in Dubai, including searches related to:

- Digital marketing agencies in Dubai
- SEO companies in Dubai
- Digital marketing agencies in Dubai Falls
- SEO companies in St. Catharines
- Local digital marketing services
- Web design and SEO providers

We then compared the types of businesses appearing prominently in traditional search with the signals that can make businesses easier for AI-powered systems to discover and understand.

This wasn't intended to reverse-engineer either Google or an AI platform.

It was designed to answer a simpler question:

Does strong traditional search visibility tell the whole story anymore?

Our observation was: not necessarily.

## Finding #1: Google Visibility and AI Visibility Are Not the Same Thing

The first important observation was that traditional search and AI recommendations shouldn't be treated as identical discovery systems.

Google's conventional results generally provide a ranked selection of pages that match the query.

An AI recommendation has a different task.

If someone asks:

> “Which digital marketing agencies should I consider in Dubai?”

the system may need to identify businesses, understand what each offers and gather enough information to formulate a useful recommendation.

This means being discoverable is only part of the challenge.

The business also needs to be understandable.

A company can have a locally optimized landing page, but that alone doesn't necessarily establish everything an AI system might want to understand about its services, reputation, expertise and relevance.

## Finding #2: Strong Businesses Leave Signals Beyond Their Homepage

One pattern became particularly interesting when examining businesses with established search visibility.

Their presence often wasn't limited to one homepage.

Search results could reveal combinations of:

service pages, location pages, reviews, business profiles, case studies, third-party references and other branded results.

This matters because someone researching a company rarely relies on one source.

AI systems can also encounter information about businesses across the wider web.

This led to one of the main lessons from our experiment:

Your website tells search systems who you say you are. Your wider digital footprint can help corroborate that story.

For local businesses, that makes reputation and entity consistency increasingly important.

## Finding #3: Reviews Matter, But They Aren’t the Entire Strategy

Reviews are clearly important for local businesses.

A strong review profile can help potential customers decide whether a company deserves their trust, and Google itself considers review signals as part of local prominence.

But our analysis suggests businesses shouldn't interpret that as:

More reviews = automatic AI visibility.

AI recommendation queries can involve broader information.

What services does the company provide?

Where does it operate?

Does its website demonstrate expertise?

Is the business mentioned elsewhere?

Are there independent sources discussing it?

Can the system confidently distinguish the business from similarly named companies?

Reviews are one component of that picture rather than the entire picture.

## Finding #4: Clear Service and Location Relationships Matter

Another noticeable difference between businesses was how clearly they communicated the relationship between their services and locations.

Consider the difference between a website that simply says:

> “We provide digital marketing.”

and one that clearly establishes:

<Flow>Business → Digital Marketing Agency → SEO → Local SEO → Web Design → Dubai</Flow>

The second creates much clearer relationships.

This is where traditional SEO and AI Search Optimization begin to overlap.

Good website architecture, descriptive service pages, consistent business information, relevant location signals and structured information can make a business easier to understand.

These practices aren't exclusively “AI SEO.”

They're good search fundamentals that may become even more useful as discovery becomes more conversational.

## Finding #5: Third-Party Visibility Deserves More Attention

One of the biggest lessons from this experiment wasn't about something businesses should add to their own websites.

It was about what happens outside them.

Search results around agencies can include company websites alongside directories, review platforms, business profiles and editorial lists.

That creates an important question for AI visibility:

What does the wider web say about the business?

For years, SEO strategies have often viewed third-party websites primarily as backlink opportunities.

AI-driven discovery gives us another reason to think about them.

A legitimate profile, local media mention, industry directory listing, expert contribution or independent review can potentially provide another source connecting:

Brand + service + location + expertise.

That doesn't mean businesses should chase hundreds of low-quality citations.

It means credible third-party presence deserves to be considered part of modern search visibility.

## So, Does Ranking on Google Mean You'll Be Recommended by AI?

Based on this exploratory test, we wouldn't make that assumption.

Strong traditional SEO provides an important foundation. A technically sound website, useful content, service relevance, local authority and backlinks still matter.

But AI recommendation visibility introduces another layer.

Businesses increasingly need to think about three connected questions:

Can search systems find us?

That's traditional SEO and technical discoverability.

Can they understand us?

That's content, website structure, entity clarity and topical relevance.

Can they find evidence beyond our own claims?

That's where reviews, reputation, third-party mentions, citations and broader brand authority become interesting.

## What Should Local Businesses Do?

Our experiment suggests businesses shouldn't abandon SEO to chase the newest AI optimization trend.

The better strategy is to expand what SEO means.

Build strong service pages.

Keep Google Business Profile information accurate.

Create genuinely useful content.

Publish first-hand experience and original information where possible.

Earn real reviews.

Build relevant third-party mentions.

Keep business, service and location information consistent.

And monitor how your brand appears not only in Google Search, but also across AI-powered discovery.

The goal is no longer simply:

> “Rank #1 for this keyword.”

A stronger long-term objective is:

Become a business that search engines and AI systems can consistently find, understand and verify.

That is where traditional SEO, Local SEO, AIO, AEO and GEO increasingly meet.
