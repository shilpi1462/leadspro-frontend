You may not need a completely new website.

Sometimes the stronger decision is to understand what is already working, what is holding customers back and what should be improved without unnecessarily rebuilding everything.

Goodfaith’s Website Redesign & CRO services combine website strategy, UX/UI improvements, conversion rate optimization, mobile usability, performance and SEO considerations to turn an underperforming website into a stronger business asset.

The goal is not simply to give your website a newer appearance.

It is to make the website clearer, easier to use and more effective at turning visitors into customers.

## What Is Website Redesign & CRO?

Website redesign improves an existing website’s structure, appearance and user experience, while Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO) focuses on helping more visitors complete valuable actions.

Those actions might include:

- Calling your business
- Requesting a quote
- Booking an appointment
- Scheduling a consultation
- Completing a form
- Making a purchase
- Signing up
- Visiting an important service page

A redesign asks:

How should this website work better?

CRO asks:

What is preventing more visitors from taking action?

Goodfaith connects the two.

## When Does a Website Need a Redesign?

A website may need more than cosmetic changes if:

- It looks noticeably outdated
- Mobile users struggle to navigate it
- Traffic is coming in but enquiries are low
- Important services are difficult to find
- Pages feel cluttered or confusing
- The brand has changed
- The site loads slowly
- Forms are too complicated
- Visitors do not know what to do next
- Important trust information is missing
- The website has grown without a clear structure
- SEO pages are poorly organized
- Competitors provide a much easier customer experience

One of the most important signs is simple:

People are visiting, but the website is not helping enough of them move forward.

## UX/UI Redesign

Visitors should be able to quickly understand:

Where am I? → What does this company offer? → Is it right for me? → What should I do next?

We may improve:

- Navigation
- Page layouts
- Content hierarchy
- Typography
- Spacing
- Buttons
- Forms
- Mobile menus
- Service presentation
- Visual consistency
- Calls to action

A cleaner interface can help, but CRO is not about making everything minimal.

The goal is to remove unnecessary friction while keeping the information customers need to make a decision.

## Conversion Rate Optimization

More traffic is not always the first solution.

If a website receives visitors but generates very few enquiries, improving the existing journey may create more value.

We review conversion factors such as:

- Is the offer clear?
- Is the next action obvious?
- Is the form unnecessarily long?
- Are calls to action placed naturally?
- Does the page answer important objections?
- Is there enough evidence to create trust?
- Is important information buried?
- Are visitors being sent to the right page?

Our basic conversion journey is:

<Flow>Need → Answer → Proof → Confidence → Action</Flow>

CRO should improve the journey—not simply add more buttons.

## Service Page & Content Restructuring

Older websites often grow without a clear plan.

You may have:

- Multiple services on one page
- Duplicate pages
- Outdated information
- Important services hidden in navigation
- Blog posts disconnected from commercial pages
- Weak internal links

A redesign gives us an opportunity to reorganize the website around how customers actually search and make decisions.

That may involve:

<Flow>Main Service → Specific Service → Supporting Information → Proof → Conversion</Flow>

This can also create a stronger foundation for future SEO.

## Mobile & Responsive Improvements

A website that works well on desktop can still create a frustrating mobile experience.

We review:

- Mobile navigation
- Forms
- Button placement
- Text size
- Content order
- Image behaviour
- Spacing
- Sticky elements
- Calls to action
- Loading experience

Mobile redesign is part of the customer journey—not a smaller version of the desktop layout.

## Website Performance Improvements

Heavy images, unnecessary scripts, overloaded plugins and poorly built page elements can make an otherwise attractive website feel slow.

Depending on the project, improvements may include:

- Image optimization
- Reducing unnecessary page weight
- Reviewing scripts
- Better loading behaviour
- Plugin cleanup
- Layout stability
- Mobile performance improvements

Performance should be considered during the redesign rather than treated as something to fix after launch.

## SEO-Safe Website Redesign

One of the biggest mistakes during a website redesign is focusing entirely on appearance and accidentally damaging existing search visibility.

Before removing, renaming or moving important pages, Goodfaith considers:

- Existing URLs
- Important ranking pages
- Internal links
- Page titles and headings
- Search Console information where available
- Redirect requirements
- Existing content value
- Indexing
- Navigation changes

A redesign should improve the website without unnecessarily throwing away search equity the business has already earned.

## The Goodfaith Website Redesign & CRO Framework

We use six stages:

- **1. Diagnose** — What is actually underperforming?
- **2. Prioritize** — Which improvements are most likely to matter?
- **3. Redesign** — How can the customer experience become clearer?
- **4. Protect** — What existing SEO, content and business value should be preserved?
- **5. Convert** — What needs to change to make customer action easier?
- **6. Improve** — What should be measured and refined after launch?

Our principle is:

Do not redesign blindly. Understand before replacing.

## Why We Don't Automatically Rebuild Everything

A redesign should preserve what already works.

If an existing service page:

- Attracts search traffic
- Generates enquiries
- Has useful backlinks
- Answers customer questions well
- Performs strongly

there may be no reason to replace it completely.

Instead, we can determine what should be:

<Flow>Keep → Improve → Combine → Remove → Rebuild</Flow>

This prevents redesign projects from becoming expensive visual resets with no clear business reason.

## Website Redesign or a Completely New Website?

| Redesign May Be Better When | New Website May Be Better When |
| --- | --- |
| Core platform still works | Existing technology is severely limiting |
| Valuable pages already exist | Website structure needs complete replacement |
| Branding needs modernization | Business has completely repositioned |
| Conversion journey is weak | Requirements have fundamentally changed |
| SEO value should be preserved | Existing website provides little reusable value |

Goodfaith reviews the existing website before recommending the larger solution.

We do not recommend a complete rebuild simply because it creates a bigger project.
