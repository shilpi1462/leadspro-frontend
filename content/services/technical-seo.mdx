Your website can have strong content, useful services and a professional design but still struggle in Google if search engines cannot properly find, understand or index its important pages.

Goodfaith’s Technical SEO services identify the website problems that can limit organic visibility and prioritize the fixes that matter most.

The goal is not to achieve a perfect score in an SEO audit tool.

It is to remove technical barriers so your important pages have a stronger foundation to compete in search.

## What Is Technical SEO?

Technical SEO improves the parts of your website that help search engines discover, access, process and index your pages correctly.

For example, you may have an excellent service page, but it will have limited value if:

- Google cannot discover it
- It has accidentally been set to noindex
- Another URL is being treated as the main version
- Important internal links are broken
- A redirect sends users and search engines somewhere incorrectly
- The page relies on technology that makes important content difficult to access
- A website redesign removed or changed valuable URLs

Technical SEO helps identify and correct problems like these.

It creates the conditions for your content and wider SEO strategy to work properly.

## How Do I Know If My Website Has Technical SEO Problems?

Technical issues are not always obvious to customers.

A website can look completely normal while search engines encounter problems behind the scenes.

Warning signs can include:

- Important pages missing from Google
- Sudden visibility loss after a redesign
- Search Console indexing errors
- Duplicate versions of pages
- Broken pages or links
- Incorrect redirects
- Very slow pages
- Important pages buried deep in the website
- Old URLs continuing to appear in search
- New pages taking unusually long to be discovered
- Unexpected pages appearing in Google

Goodfaith investigates why the issue is happening before recommending the fix.

## Canonicals, Redirects & Duplicate URLs

Websites can sometimes create multiple URLs containing the same or very similar information.

We review areas such as:

- Canonical tags
- HTTP and HTTPS versions
- WWW and non-WWW versions
- Redirect chains
- Broken redirects
- Legacy URLs
- Parameter URLs
- Duplicate pages

The objective is to make it clearer which URL should represent the content.

Your internal links, redirects, sitemap and canonical signals should not be telling different stories.

Goodfaith reviews whether important content remains accessible and whether a simpler implementation could achieve the same customer experience.

## The Goodfaith Technical SEO Framework

We simplify technical SEO into five questions:

- **Access** — Can search engines reach the page?
- **Index** — Can the correct page appear in search?
- **Consolidate** — Are redirects, canonicals and duplicate URLs pointing in the right direction?
- **Experience** — Does the website perform properly for customers across devices?
- **Validate** — Did the implemented fix actually solve the problem?

This gives us a practical rule:

### Blockers Before Boosters

A technical audit can produce hundreds of warnings.

They are not all equally important.

Goodfaith generally prioritizes:

1. Crawl blockers

Can Google reach important pages?

2. Indexing blockers

Can those pages appear in search?

3. Conflicting signals

Are redirects, duplicates or canonicals creating confusion?

4. Discovery problems

Are important pages poorly connected?

5. Performance issues

Is technical performance creating meaningful customer friction?

6. **Enhancements** — Are there useful improvements that can be made after the major problems are resolved?

We focus first on issues that can affect important pages and business opportunities.
