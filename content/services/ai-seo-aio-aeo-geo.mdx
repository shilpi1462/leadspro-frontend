## What Is AI SEO?

AI SEO is the process of improving how clearly your business, website and information can be discovered and understood across AI-powered search experiences.

It does not replace traditional SEO.

Google says its generative AI experiences remain rooted in its existing Search ranking and quality systems.

Strong AI-search readiness still starts with:

- A crawlable and indexable website
- Clear service pages
- Useful, original information
- Strong internal linking
- Real expertise
- Consistent business information
- Credible external references
- Technical performance
- Website authority

AI SEO adds greater attention to:

- Conversational questions
- Clear answers

- Original evidence
- Citation-worthy information
- AI-search visibility measurement

## What Do AIO, AEO and GEO Mean?

Several terms are now used to describe optimization for AI-powered discovery.

### AIO — Artificial Intelligence Optimization

AIO is commonly used as a broad umbrella term for improving how information is understood and discovered by AI-powered systems.

### AEO — Answer Engine Optimization

AEO focuses on making useful answers clear and easy for search and answer-driven systems to interpret.

### GEO — Generative Engine Optimization

GEO focuses on visibility within generative experiences that construct answers using information retrieved from multiple sources.

There is substantial overlap between these ideas.

Google itself says that, from Google Search’s perspective, work described as AEO or GEO is still fundamentally SEO for the evolving search experience.

LeadsPro therefore focuses on the practical question:

> Can customers and modern search systems find clear, useful and trustworthy information about your business?

## How Is AI Search Different From Traditional Search?

Traditional search often begins with a short keyword.

Traditional search:

> “commercial cleaning company Vancouver”

Conversational search might look like:

> “What should I look for when choosing a commercial cleaning company in Vancouver for a 50-person office?”

That question involves much more than one keyword.

The customer may care about:

- Services
- Office size
- Cleaning frequency
- Insurance
- Staff screening
- Availability
- Pricing
- Reviews
- Location

Modern SEO therefore needs to understand the whole customer decision, not just one phrase.

## One Question Can Trigger Multiple Searches

Google uses a technique called query fan-out within its generative Search experiences.

A complex question can generate multiple related searches so Google can retrieve additional information about different parts of the topic before creating a response.

For example:

> “Which basement waterproofing company near me should I hire?”

could involve related searches around:

- Services
- Location
- Waterproofing methods
- Reviews
- Experience
- Pricing
- Relevant sources

This does not mean businesses should create a separate page for every possible fan-out query.

Google specifically warns against creating large numbers of pages for every search variation simply to influence rankings or generative responses.
