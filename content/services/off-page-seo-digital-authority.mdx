## What Is Off-Page SEO?

Off-Page SEO is the work done outside your own website to strengthen its authority, reputation and connections across the web.

The best-known part of Off-Page SEO is backlinks—links from other websites to yours.

But modern Off-Page SEO can also include:

- Business citations
- Industry mentions
- Digital PR
- Professional directories
- Partner references
- Local business listings
- Expert contributions
- Brand mentions
- Link reclamation
- Original research that earns references
- Useful resources other websites can recommend

Google uses links as one of many signals for understanding relationships between pages and evaluating the web.

Off-Page SEO is therefore not simply about acquiring links.

It is about building credible connections around the real business.

## Why Can Competitors Outrank a Strong Website?

A competitor may have similar services and a similar website but a much stronger presence across the wider web.

They may already have:

- Relevant industry websites referring to them
- Trusted business citations
- Professional or local organization listings
- Editorial mentions
- Partnership references
- Research or guides that other websites cite
- Links pointing directly to important service pages
- A stronger history of legitimate online references

The answer is not automatically to copy every backlink they have.

LeadsPro first asks:

- Who is referencing the competitor?
- Why are they referencing them?
- Would that same type of reference make sense for your business?

This helps separate genuine opportunities from backlink noise.

## Backlink Quality Matters More Than Backlink Quantity

A large backlink count does not automatically mean stronger authority.

LeadsPro evaluates external references around:

<Flow sep="•">Relevance • Credibility • Context • Audience Value • Integrity</Flow>

A smaller number of legitimate references from sources connected to your industry, location, audience or expertise can be more strategically useful than large numbers of unrelated links.

### Our principle:

Build references that make sense for the business—not backlinks simply to increase a number in an SEO tool.

## How LeadsPro Decides Whether a Backlink Is Worth Pursuing

We use a simple five-part check:

<Flow>Relevance → Credibility → Context → Audience Value → Integrity</Flow>

1. **Is the Website Relevant?** — A reference should have a logical connection to your business.

For example, a cybersecurity company being mentioned by a technology publication makes sense.

The same company appearing on a random website publishing unrelated articles about gambling, gardening, insurance and software simply for backlinks deserves much more scrutiny.

## 2. Is the Website Credible?

We look beyond third-party authority scores.

We ask:

- Is this a genuine website?
- Does it publish useful information?
- Does it appear to serve a real audience?
- Does the content have a clear purpose?
- Would we be comfortable associating the client’s brand with it?

A simple LeadsPro test is:

> Would we still value this mention if the SEO metric beside the website disappeared?

## 3. Why Does the Link Exist?

A good reference normally has a reason.

Perhaps:

- An expert was quoted
- Research was cited
- A guide was recommended
- A partnership was mentioned
- A useful tool was included
- The business belongs to an organization
- A publisher found a resource genuinely useful

A backlink should make sense to a reader—not only to an SEO tool.

## 4. Could the Reference Reach the Right People?

A smaller niche publication read by potential customers may sometimes provide more strategic value than a large but unrelated website.

That is why we consider audience relevance, not simply website size.

## 5. Was the Link Acquired Legitimately?

Google’s spam policies warn against practices such as:

- Buying links for ranking purposes
- Automated link creation
- Excessive link exchanges
- Low-quality directory links created primarily to manipulate rankings

LeadsPro avoids backlink strategies built around those shortcuts.

## Citations: An Important Part of Digital Authority

A citation is an online reference to a business, often including information such as:

- Business name
- Address where applicable
- Phone number
- Website
- Services
- Business information

Relevant citations can help create a more consistent external footprint for a business.

LeadsPro looks for quality citation opportunities rather than submitting businesses to hundreds of random directories.

We also pay attention to consistency.

Where appropriate:

<Flow>Website Information → Business Profiles → Citations → External References</Flow>

should communicate the same real-world business information.

One pattern from our applied SEO work is that external references are more useful when they reinforce the same factual business identity rather than creating conflicting information.

## How Original Content Can Help Earn Better Backlinks

The easiest content to create is often the hardest content to earn links to.

Compare:

> “10 Benefits of SEO.”

Thousands of websites can publish that.

Now compare it with:

> “What We Found After Auditing 50 Small-Business Websites in Calgary.”

The second gives another publisher something original to discuss.

LeadsPro looks for link-worthy opportunities such as:

- Original research
- Industry surveys
- Real data
- Expert observations
- Useful calculators
- Checklists
- Local market studies
- Original frameworks
- Before-and-after findings
- Practical benchmarks
- Detailed decision guides

A stronger authority strategy becomes:

<Flow>Experience → Research → Useful Resource → Outreach → Natural Reference</Flow>

rather than:

<Flow>Generic Article → Ask Everyone for a Link</Flow>
